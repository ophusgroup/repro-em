# Learning from bio TEM

The biological electron microscopy community has been enormously successful at defining shared deposition and validation standards through open-access archives.

For structural biology, the PDB and the EMDB provide stable identifiers, community metadata expectations, and established validation pathways that make results reusable and comparable across laboratories ([Rose et al., *Nucleic Acids Res.* 2017](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkw1000); [wwPDB Consortium, *Nucleic Acids Res.* 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkad1019)). For cryo-electron tomography, the same philosophy extends upstream to raw and intermediate data, with EMPIAR and the CryoET Data Portal enabling broad reprocessing, method development, and independent verification on shared datasets ([Iudin et al., *Nucleic Acids Res.* 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkac1062); [Ermel et al., *Nat. Methods* 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-024-02477-2)).

These resources go far beyond simply storing the data. They normalized the idea that preservation and validation are essential parts of characterization science.

## What actually made it work

The archives are the visible part, and they are not the reason the system holds. Three other things did the work.

**Deposition became a condition of publication.** Journals will not print a structure without an accession code. This is the single most important mechanism on this page, and it is enforced by editors rather than by goodwill.

**Validation is automatic and public.** A deposited structure receives a validation report generated by the archive, not by the authors, and that report travels with the entry. Reviewers read it. Nobody has to trust a methods section.

**One agreed number.** Resolution by FSC, computed the same way for every entry, means any two results in the archive can be compared. See [benchmarks](framework/benchmarks.md).

## What does not transfer

Bio EM converges on a small number of questions with a shared answer format, which is what makes a single schema and a single metric workable. Materials TEM does not have that, and pretending otherwise is how previous standardization efforts have failed. See [materials science TEM](materials-tem.md).

What does transfer is the structure of the solution: mandatory deposition tied to publication, validation computed by someone other than the author, and metrics the whole community agrees to report. None of those require a single experiment type.
