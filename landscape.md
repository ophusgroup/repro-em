# TEM landscape

Materials electron microscopy is produced by three groups with different incentives, and any proposal that ignores one of them will fail. Academic researchers generate most of the published data and carry most of the curation burden. Industrial laboratories run a large fraction of the world's instruments and publish very little of what they record. Vendors control the instruments, the detectors, and the file formats, and therefore control what is technically possible for everyone else.

The reason reproducibility has not improved on its own is that the cost falls on one group while the benefit accrues to another. A researcher who curates a dataset properly spends days of their own time so that a stranger can check their work. An instrument that writes complete metadata costs the vendor engineering effort to benefit a customer who has not asked for it. Nothing in the present arrangement rewards any actor for doing the right thing.

This is why the recommendations on this site are addressed to specific actors rather than to the field in general, and why several of them are requirements rather than suggestions. See [calls to action](calls-to-action.md).
