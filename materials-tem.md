# Materials science TEM

Materials science TEM produces far more heterogeneous data than biological TEM. This is because materials research employs a much wider array of methods, and covers essentially all kinds of materials from alloys to 2D materials to polymers to liquids.

That heterogeneity is the reason our field has no equivalent of the PDB. Biological SPA converges on one question, the 3D structure of an averaged particle, so a single deposition schema and a single validation metric serve most of the field. Materials TEM asks a different question in nearly every experiment. We measure where the average structure breaks: a dopant atom, a dislocation core, a grain boundary, a phase front moving under applied bias. The measurement that answers one of those questions is often useless for the next.

Standardization is therefore harder here than in structural biology, and still necessary. Whatever the experiment, a reader needs the same three things to check our work: the raw detector output, enough metadata to interpret it, and the code that turned one into the other.
