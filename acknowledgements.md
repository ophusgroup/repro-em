# Acknowledgements

This site accompanies an invited talk at the Microscopy & Microanalysis 2026 meeting, given by [Colin Ophus](https://colab.stanford.edu/) of the Materials Science and Engineering Department at Stanford University.

The arguments here were shaped by many people across the electron microscopy community, and by the open source projects that make quantitative electron microscopy possible. Particular thanks to the members of the COLab group at Stanford, and to collaborators at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, UC Berkeley, TU Delft, the University of Erlangen-Nürnberg, and the many other institutions represented in this work.

Several of the ideas on this site are drawn from communities the author is not part of. The structural biology archives, and the crystallographers who built and maintain the CIF standard and checkCIF, worked out most of this decades ago. This site is largely an argument that materials electron microscopy should pay attention to what they did.

## Software

The tools discussed throughout this site are open source and community maintained, including [quantEM](https://github.com/electronmicroscopy/quantem), py4DSTEM, abTEM, Prismatic, HyperSpy, and RosettaSciIO, along with the Zarr and HDF5 projects that underpin them.

This site is built with [MyST](https://mystmd.org/), and is published under a [Creative Commons Attribution 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) license. Corrections, additions, and disagreements are welcome through the [repository](https://github.com/ophusgroup/repro-em).
