# Industry

Industrial laboratories operate a large share of the world's electron microscopes, and very little of what they record is published. Semiconductor manufacturers, battery and materials companies, and pharmaceutical laboratories run instruments continuously, often on samples and at a throughput that academic groups cannot match.

Most of that data is genuinely restricted, and no framework that pretends otherwise will be adopted. Process details are competitive, some materials are export controlled, and patent filings impose timing constraints that are not negotiable.

Two things are still possible under those constraints. Methods, calibration procedures, and analysis code can usually be published even when the specimens cannot, and a reader who can inspect the method is in a much better position than one who has neither. And where data is released, an embargo with a fixed expiry date and a registered identifier is compatible with everything on this site, while an indefinite restriction with no expiry is not.

Industry also has a direct interest in the outcome. A laboratory that cannot reproduce its own measurement from two years ago has a business problem rather than a philosophical one, and the instrument metadata and provenance tracking this site argues for solve that internal problem first. Industrial customers are also the ones with enough purchasing power to make vendors implement open formats and signed detector output. See [calls to action](../calls-to-action.md).
