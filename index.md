# Open Formats, Standard Metadata, and Benchmarks

A reproducibility framework for materials electron microscopy.

Materials electron microscopy includes an unusually broad range of samples, measurements, and data dimensionalities. We study metals, ceramics, polymers, nanoparticles, 2D materials, and semiconductors using imaging, diffraction, spectroscopy, tomography, and *in situ* methods, and we record datasets ranging from 2D images to 5D momentum-resolved spectrum images. Many experiments are designed to measure local deviations from the average structure, including individual dopant atoms, dislocation cores, interfaces, and phase fronts moving under an applied field.

This breadth has made community-wide standardization difficult, and materials electron microscopy does not yet have a deposition and validation resource comparable to the Protein Data Bank. Structural biology and crystallography accommodated different experimental methods while converging on deposition tied to publication, validation performed by someone other than the author, and a common set of reported metrics. Materials electron microscopy can adopt these practices because they do not depend on a common measurement.

The repro-EM framework follows the data through six stages, from the detector to the published figure:

1. **[Signed detector output](framework/detectors.md).** Detectors should cryptographically sign a hash of the raw output together with the acquisition metadata.
2. **[Immutable raw data](framework/data.md).** Raw data must be identified by a content hash and persistent identifier, and stored in an open, self-describing format with a documented, versioned layout specification.
3. **[Calibration and metadata](framework/metadata.md).** Calibrations must be recorded at acquisition with their value, method, uncertainty, and date, together with the sample and instrument metadata required to interpret the measurement.
4. **[Reconstruction or reduction](framework/computational-imaging.md).** Each reconstruction must record its code version, parameters, and random seeds, and inverse problems must be tested against held-out measurements.
5. **[Analysis](framework/analysis.md).** Analysis software must be open source, use a pinned environment, and declare all inputs and transformations.
6. **[Figure generation](framework/chain-of-custody.md).** Every figure must be generated by a script, with hashes linking each derived product to its inputs so that the figure can be traced back to signed detector output.

Existing data containers, metadata systems, version-control tools, cross-validation methods, and cryptographic signatures are sufficient to implement this chain, and the remaining task is to define and adopt the community standards needed to use them.
